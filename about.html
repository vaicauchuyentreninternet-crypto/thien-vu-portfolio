A short biography

I followed the friction.

I started close to the day-to-day work: preparing products, handling catalog details, and seeing how much time the same small tasks could take. One product could take roughly an hour. That seemed like a question worth asking.

So I began writing down the steps, spotting the decisions that kept repeating, and turning the predictable parts into a workflow. The reported processing time went from around 60 minutes to under seven per product. What stayed with me was the method, not just the number.

As the systems grew, I wanted to make them easier to inspect. That led to an interactive GenLogin explorer mapping 19 workflows and 2,489 nodes, and a SKU decoder that made product identifiers less opaque. A system feels different when someone else can follow its logic.

These days, I'm drawn to applied AI and web interfaces that make complicated work feel manageable. I like tools with clear rules, useful feedback, and room for a human to make the final call.
