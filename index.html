I kept asking why one product took an hour.

Automation engineering · Applied AI · Web systems

That first question led me from operations into automation, applied AI, and interfaces that make complex systems usable.

The tools came later.
The problem came first.

I didn't start by wanting to make a clever demo. I wanted work to move without asking someone to make the same decision over and over. The more I built, the more I cared about making the result understandable.

ACT 01 THE FRICTION

The hour nobody had.

One product could take 60 minutes to process. Multiply that across a catalog, and the problem wasn't a slow person. It was a process asking people to repeat the same work.

Starting point60 min per productReported daily volume: 200+ products
ACT 02 THE METHOD

First, I drew the messy middle.

I started turning repeated decisions into rules and tracing where the workflow needed clarity. The goal was not to automate everything. It was to understand what should happen consistently.

Catalog scope2,000+ products classified1,600+ variants
ACT 03 THE SCALE

One fix revealed the next bottleneck.

As the work expanded, I needed ways to see and explain increasingly complex automation. Mapping the system became as important as building it.

GenLogin explorer19 workflows2,489 mapped nodes
ACT 04 THE DIRECTION

Now I design the way in.

Today, I build tools and interfaces that help people understand the systems behind their work. A good result should be useful, inspectable, and possible to improve.
